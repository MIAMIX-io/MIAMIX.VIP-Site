In 2018, in Miami, Florida, David Beckham, Jorge Mas, and José Mas met for a conversation. It wasn't just any conversation: it was the beginning of a dream. They wondered how it was possible that a city with so much energy, diversity, and love for soccer didn't have a club that represented that passion. That void transformed into a plan, and that plan into a mission: to found a team that would be a symbol of unity, pride, and hope.

From the very beginning, Inter Miami was destined to make a difference. Its first jersey, pink, broke with all the established conventions. The color no one dared to wear became the flag, accompanied by a phrase that seemed like a declaration of principles: Freedom to dream. The future was rosy, and although the club was just starting, it already conveyed the feeling of making history.

A stadium was built to begin dreaming big, but the pandemic arrived. The games stopped, the world stopped, but the dreams didn't. Inter Miami overcame that obstacle with the conviction that soccer is much more than ninety minutes: it's community, identity, and hope.

The first results weren't easy: five straight losses. But in the sixth game, against Orlando, the first victory came, and with it, the birth of a rivalry that ignited the fans. Little by little, thousands of people joined the cause. Immigrants from all over the world found in the club the emotional refuge they had lost when leaving their countries. Inter Miami ceased to be a sports project and became a family of dreamers.

In just four years, the transformation was astonishing. The jersey sold out, as did the tickets, and the club began to inspire passion far beyond Florida. From Buenos Aires to Shanghai, children and young people began to identify with the pink of Miami. The arrival of figures like Sergio Busquets and Jordi Alba gave it a status, but the emergence of Lionel Messi transformed the club into a global phenomenon.

The journey wasn't without drama, epic comebacks, and memorable goals that thrilled the stadium and the entire world. Young players from the academy demonstrated that the club's essence lies in developing new generations. With effort and football, Inter Miami shattered the prejudices of those who doubted the league and proved that the dream was real.

Four years later, the symbol of growth became tangible: the small plastic cup of the early days was replaced by the first official trophy. A silver trophy that not only represents a sporting title, but also the realization of that shared dream in 2018. And as is often the case with dreams fulfilled, this one merely paved the way for many more.
